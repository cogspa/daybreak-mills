Process

How art gets onto a box

The loop is flat art in, rendered carton out. Every step below points at a file that already exists in your cereal_box/ folder.

  1. Open the dieline template for the size you're designing

    templates/dieline_Regular.svg — panels, fold lines, safe areas and the front-panel grid, each on its own named layer.

  2. Design on top of it, keeping the template layers locked

    Artwork bleeds to the panel edge. Live matter — type, logos, anything you'd mind losing — stays inside the dashed 4 mm line.

  3. Export flat at 4096 × 4096, PNG, no transparency

    Hide the template layers first. The canvas must stay square and full-bleed; the panel rectangles are positions in UV space, not a crop.

  4. Wire it into the box material through the Dieline UV

    Image Texture → Base Color, with a UV Map node set to Dieline. Not CubeProject — that one overlaps front against back.

  5. Render and judge it at shelf distance

    Everything reads at 40 cm. The real test is 2 m, at a glance, next to five siblings. That's what the 24-box grid scene is for.

Anatomy

The dieline

Six faces unfolded into one square canvas: front, right, back and left in a horizontal strip, with the top and bottom flaps hinged off the front. Proportions below are the real Regular carton.

grey area outside the net is dead UV space — nothing there is ever rendered

Two UV maps, and you want the second one.

CubeProject is the box unwrap — clean, even texel density, but front and back share the same UV region by design, so anything you paint on one appears mirrored on the other. Use it for tiling materials like kraft or a paper grain. Dieline gives every face its own non-overlapping island. That's the one artwork goes through.

All four sizes share one square canvas, so the effective print resolution changes with how much carton has to fit into it. Numbers are for the front panel.

4096 is fine for renders — 162 to 200 DPI holds up well past the distance anyone views a render from. It is not print resolution. Mega is the weak one, because it packs the most carton into the same square; if a layout has to survive both a render and a press, author it at 8192 and downsample for Blender. Mini is the outlier in the other direction: a small carton in a full-size canvas lands at 372 DPI, effectively print-ready already.

Identity

The masthead sits on a bone chip. Always.

DAYBREAK MILLS
Mill Navy #16233A on Bone #F4EFE6

Six flavour fields means six different backgrounds for one logo, and no single ink survives all of them. Navy on Cocoa Comets' brown is unreadable; navy on Frosted Stars' blue is worse — the two are within a few degrees of hue.

So the mark never sits directly on the flavour field. It always rides a bone chip, which gives it the same contrast on every one of the six. That single rule is what makes the range read as a family on shelf.

Clear space and size

Clear space on all four sides equals the cap height of the wordmark. Minimum chip width is 55 mm on Regular, Family and Mega; 34 mm on Mini. The chip is a rectangle with a 2 mm corner radius — never a rounded pill, never knocked out to a silhouette.

The nutrition panel is regulated, and not the way most people assume.

The FDA does not mandate Helvetica — that's convention, not law. What it does require is a typeface that isn't decorative, script or stylised, plus minimum sizes: the calorie amount at 22 pt, the word "Calories" at 16 pt (10 pt allowed on small packages), servings and serving size at 10 pt, and "Amount Per Serving", "%DV" and the footnote at 6 pt with 1 pt leading. "Nutrition Facts" must be larger than everything else in the panel except the calorie figure. Design the right panel to those numbers from the start — it is the one panel you cannot resolve by eye.

Layout

Front panel zones

Mastheadrows 1–2
Flavour namerows 3–4
Product shotrows 5–11
Claim flashrows 12–13
Net weightrows 14–16

The template carries a 12 × 16 grid on the front panel. Columns handle horizontal rhythm; the sixteen rows split into five fixed zones, and every flavour uses the same five. That consistency is the whole point — swap the field colour and the product shot, and the range still reads as one family from across an aisle.

Rules that don't bend

  • Masthead never moves. Same rows, same width, same chip on all six.
  • One claim flash. If there are two things to say, the second one goes on the left panel.
  • Product shot floats. No plate, no table edge, no drop shadow onto the field.
  • Net weight bottom-right, always in Mill Navy, always Figtree 600.
  • The field colour bleeds. No white border, no frame, no keyline around the panel.
Pre-flight

Before you export

  • Template layers hidden — fold lines, safe areas and the grid must not ship in the texture.
  • Canvas still 4096 × 4096, square, opaque. Cropping to the net breaks the UV alignment.
  • Artwork bleeds past every fold line into the neighbouring panel by at least 3 mm.
  • Nothing live outside the dashed line — 4 mm in from every fold and trim.
  • Top and bottom flaps carry art, even if it's just flat field colour. A white flap is instantly visible in a render.
  • The back panel reads right way up when the box is turned over, not mirrored.
  • UV Map node set to Dieline in the Blender material, not CubeProject.
  • Colour space set to sRGB on the Image Texture node — Blender guesses Non-Color for some PNGs.
